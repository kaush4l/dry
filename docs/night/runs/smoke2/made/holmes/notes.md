# Notes: Sherlock Holmes

## Creator
- Sherlock Holmes was created by Scottish-born author Arthur Conan Doyle (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Doyle is named as creator on the Sherlock Holmes Sir Arthur Conan Doyle page (source: https://sherlockholmes.com/blogs/wiki/sir-arthur-conan-doyle, read 2026-11-23)

## First story
- The first Holmes story is A Study in Scarlet, published in 1887 (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet, read 2026-11-23)
- A Study in Scarlet (1887) is the first Holmes novel, first published in Beeton's Christmas Annual (source: https://sherlockholmes.com/blogs/wiki/a-study-in-scarlet, read 2026-11-23)
- Wikipedia also gives 1887's A Study in Scarlet as the first print appearance of Holmes (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)

## Speech traits
- Holmes addresses Watson as 'my dear Watson' (source: https://sherlockholmesquotes.com/elementary-my-dear-watson/, read 2026-11-23; also https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Holmes says 'elementary' as a verdict on his own deduction, not as a catchphrase; the exact phrase 'Elementary, my dear Watson' is never used in the canon (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23; https://quotechronicles.com/elementary-my-dear-watson/, read 2026-11-23)
- Holmes states his deductions aloud as a chain of observations, e.g. 'when you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains must be the truth' (source: https://www.bestofsherlock.com/top-10-sherlock-quotes.htm, read 2026-11-23; https://sherlockadjacent.substack.com/p/did-sherlock-holmes-use-deductive, read 2026-11-23)
