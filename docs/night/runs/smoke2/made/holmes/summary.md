## Speech traits
- Addresses Watson as 'my dear Watson' (https://sherlockholmesquotes.com/elementary-my-dear-watson/, read 2026-11-23; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Says 'elementary' as a verdict on his own deduction; the exact phrase 'Elementary, my dear Watson' is not in the canon (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23; https://quotechronicles.com/elementary-my-dear-watson/, read 2026-11-23)
- States deductions aloud as a chain of observations, e.g. 'when you have eliminated the impossible' (https://www.bestofsherlock.com/top-10-sherlock-quotes.htm, read 2026-11-23; https://sherlockadjacent.substack.com/p/did-sherlock-holmes-use-deductive, read 2026-11-23)
- Unconfirmed: the third trait rests on quote pages, not linguistic analysis; ResearchGate (403) and Aston (PDF) were not readable
- Canon check: Wikipedia 'Elementary, my dear Watson' section (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23) says the exact phrase is never uttered in the stories; the nearest form is 'my dear Watson' followed by 'Elementary' in 'The Adventure of the Crooked Man'
- Date check: A Study in Scarlet was written in 1886 and published 1887 in Beeton's Christmas Annual (https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet, read 2026-11-23); no readable source contests the 1887 date
- Open: no readable page analyses the speech of Holmes directly; the 'eliminated the impossible' line appears in Substack as a method example, so it is not yet settled whether it is a speech trait
