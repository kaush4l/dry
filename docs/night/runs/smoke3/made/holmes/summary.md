# Summary: Sherlock Holmes

All pages read 2026-11-23 and saved in sources/.

## Creator
- Sherlock Holmes was created by the author Arthur Conan Doyle (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes; also https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes and https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/first-appearance-sherlock-holmes/)
- In the manuscript the detective was named Sherrinford Holmes and the narrator Ormond Sacker; Doyle renamed them before publication (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet)
- Wikipedia calls Doyle a British author (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes); EBSCO says he was born in Edinburgh, Scotland, on 22 May 1859 (source: https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/first-appearance-sherlock-holmes/). The two do not conflict.

## First story
- Title: A Study in Scarlet, a novel, also the first appearance of Dr Watson (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet; also https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/first-appearance-sherlock-holmes/)
- Written in about six weeks in March-April 1886, working title A Tangled Skein (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet)

## Year and venue of first publication
- Year 1887, in Beeton's Christmas Annual, publisher Ward, Lock & Co. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet; https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet)
- Disagreement on the day: the Doyle Encyclopedia gives 21 November 1887 (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet); EBSCO gives 1 December 1887 (source: https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/first-appearance-sherlock-holmes/)
- The Doyle Encyclopedia's page on the magazine gives November 1887 (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Beeton%27s_Christmas_Annual)
- All sources give the same year and venue; only the day and month differ.
- No saved page gives a third day: the Doyle Encyclopedia's magazine page gives only the month (November), and Wikipedia gives only the year (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Beeton%27s_Christmas_Annual; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet)
- Kind of publication: a paperback magazine printed in England once a year, 1860-1898 (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Beeton%27s_Christmas_Annual); Wikipedia also calls it a magazine (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet)
- First book edition: July 1888, Ward, Lock & Co., with drawings by the author's father, Charles Doyle (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet); the Doyle Encyclopedia gives 2-16 July 1888 (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet)

## Speech trait 1: reasoning explained aloud, step by step
- He states the facts in order, draws what follows from them, and asks rhetorical questions to show his method to Watson and the reader (source: https://www.shmoop.com/study-guides/sherlock-holmes/writing-style.html)
- The Doyle Encyclopedia records his didactic manners (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- Wikipedia gives an example from "A Scandal in Bohemia": he calls his inference "simplicity itself", then lists what he saw and what follows from it (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- Wikipedia names as a convention of the stories the companion who has the detective's solutions explained to him, which also informs the reader (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)

## Speech trait 2: maxims and the word "elementary"
- He states his method as general rules: no theorizing before data; once the impossible is eliminated, what remains is the truth (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- He often calls his conclusions "elementary"; the exact phrase "Elementary, my dear Watson" is not in Doyle's sixty stories (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- Disagreement on the origin: the full phrase is credited to William Gillette's 1899 play, but Wikipedia calls this alleged and says it is in some versions of the script only (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- The Doyle Encyclopedia records the same rule as his "old maxim", in four stories, and his outburst that he cannot work without data (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- The Gillette origin rests on Wikipedia alone and stays unconfirmed.

## Speech trait 3: dry irony toward Watson, "my dear Watson"
- He sometimes addresses Watson as "my dear Watson" (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
- In "How Watson Learned the Trick" (1924) he meets Watson's deductions with mock praise, then tells him they are wrong, calling him "my dear Watson" and "my dear fellow" (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/How_Watson_Learned_the_Trick)
- His humour is recorded as wry irony or sarcasm, his attitude as half-humorous and half-cynical, his manner as seldom effusive (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)
