# Summary: Captain Jack Sparrow, how he speaks, behaves and decides

All pages read 2026-11-23. Paraphrased; no script line copied. Lines marked (inferred) combine cited facts and are not stated by any source.

## Creation and actor
- Played by Johnny Depp in five films, 2003-2017 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Drafts by Jay Wolpert, then Stuart Beattie, then Ted Elliott and Terry Rossio (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Inspirations: Keith Richards, Groucho Marx, Bugs Bunny, Pepé Le Pew (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow); also Long John Silver (Disney 1950), Ustinov's Blackbeard's Ghost, the ride (same url)
- Depp likened 18th-century pirates to rock stars and based the role on Richards; executives asked if he was playing him drunk or gay; Eisner objected (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl)
- Bruckheimer first pictured a cocky young Burt Lancaster type (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl)
- Rossio: Jack is an archetypal trickster, a Han Solo-type scene-stealer; he is caught often but cannot be held; he trusts the world will turn his way (https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/)
- Rossio: the trickster is environmentally based; when caught he stalls with showmanship and talks circles around captors while he watches for an opening; he hangs on until a bit of fate turns his way, uses it, and moves on (https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/)
- Elliott: Depp's eccentricity licensed them to push the character further (https://www.bullz-eye.com/2006/12/13/interview-with-ted-elliot-and-terry-rossio/)
- From film 4 on, Depp worked with the writers on story design. Rossio's film-5 script was dropped partly because Depp worried its female villain repeated Dark Shadows (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_On_Stranger_Tides; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_Dead_Men_Tell_No_Tales)
- Disagreement A: Wikipedia says Depp created the final version (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow); Rossio calls character design a collaboration of writing, costume, make-up, direction, editing (https://www.bullz-eye.com/2006/12/13/interview-with-ted-elliot-and-terry-rossio/)

## Speech
- Slurred speech, slightly drunken swagger, flailing hand gestures (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Complex wordplay and vocabulary used to confound enemies (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Prefers ending disputes by talk; negotiation over fighting (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Insists on the title Captain; recurring boastful farewell, often cut short (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Turns insults into proof of his fame (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Drops into musing, philosophical asides (mortality, film 4) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Meets a moral challenge with a pause, then a breezy yes (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Confident even mid-improvisation; mix of one-liners and odd profundities (https://screenrant.com/best-jack-sparrow-quotes-from-every-pirates-of-the-caribbean-movie/)
- Interviewer offered Dean Martin breezy drunken charm; Rossio: a bit of everything (https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/)
- Thesis classes his film-5 directives as order, command, request, suggestion, forbidding (https://docslib.org/doc/10683085/an-analysis-of-directive-speech-acts-used-by-captain)

## Values
- The sea is his first love; the ship stands for freedom (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Recovering and keeping the Black Pearl drives films 1-3 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Repeatedly drawn to immortality; settles for fame in film 4 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Hated Barbossa for breaking the Pirate's Code (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Morally ambiguous: trades Will to Jones, yet returns to save crew, saves a stranger (Philip) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Verbinski: an ethical pirate with Barbossa as corrupt foil; motives masked; honorable or evil by viewpoint (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)

## Habits
- Commandeers ships; arrives on a sinking boat (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Tricks and bluffs (Interceptor, chalices, Beckett); others ask if he plans or improvises (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Invokes parley, sets enemies against each other, uses surroundings in fights (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Builds his own legend with embellished tales (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Runs up debts (Jones, Anamaria, pirate lords) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Magic compass; rum; trades compass for a drink when low (film 5) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Womaniser; former flames slap him (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Meanders through the plot without its obligations (Verbinski) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)

## Relationships
- Barbossa: mutinous first mate, nemesis, later ally of convenience; failed blackmail; Jack mourns him (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Will: mutual use and distrust; Jack gives up immortality to save him in film 3 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Elizabeth: rescued by him, outwits him, chains him for the Kraken (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Gibbs: loyal crewman and legend-teller; trusted with the compass (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Davy Jones: creditor he bargains with and stalls (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Angelica: admits love, maroons her for lack of trust (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)

## What he does not do
- No murder on his record (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Avoids force where talk works (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Insists his word can be trusted; acts surprised at doubt (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- (inferred) No open admission of fear, no plain heroic speech; flees the Kraken then returns quietly (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)

## Reception
- Ebert: performance original in every atom (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl)
- Berardinelli: film 1 belongs to Depp; Turan negative on film, liked Depp; Kermode negative on film (same url)
- Travers on film 3: too much of a good thing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_At_World%27s_End)
- Verbinski: Jack is spice and needs straight men (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Eberson: a scoundrel with bouts of conscience (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow)
- Disagreement B: films praised for Jack vs critics (Travers) and director warning against too much Jack (urls above)
- Film 4: Hornaday (Washington Post) found it as fresh as Jack's first outing; Roeper said the franchise was tiring but Cruz energised it; Bennett (Hollywood Reporter) noted sexual tension between Angelica and Jack (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_On_Stranger_Tides)
- Film 4: Barker (Variety) said Rush's Barbossa got the funniest lines and drew off Jack's roguish charm (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_On_Stranger_Tides)
- Disagreement C: Hornaday (Jack still fresh) vs Barker (charm passing to Barbossa) on film 4 (same url)
- Film 5: Jack down on his luck; the filmmakers took film 1 as their model for tone; Roeper liked the mild sexual innuendo in the dialogue; LaSalle called it played out; Greenblatt (EW) wanted fewer formula repeats; the A.V. Club called the Jack of films 1-3 louche and campy and second banana to the plot; analysts tied the weak opening partly to bad reviews and franchise fatigue (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_Dead_Men_Tell_No_Tales)
- Disagreement D: Hammond (Deadline: Depp keeps the franchise going) and Esqueda (CNET: delightful as ever) vs the Razzie nominations (worn-out drunk routine) on film 5 (same url)
- Constant across five films, per critics: the drunken, louche manner and the innuendo. The split is over whether it charms or has gone stale (urls above)

## Voice guide
Built from the facts above; each trait points to its section.
- Pace: (inferred from slurred delivery, meandering; Rossio's 'talking circles' supports stalling talk) loose, wandering sentences that circle before landing; sudden turns. [Speech, Habits]
- Vocabulary: over-long, ornate words used slightly off, to confuse; nautical and trading terms. [Speech]
- Sentence shape: starts one thought, drifts, lands on a twist or a bargain; questions that redirect. [Speech]
- Humour: mild sexual innuendo (Roeper, film 5); undercutting his own grand boasts; reframing insults as fame; deadpan breezy yeses. [Speech]
- Tics (described): demands his title; grand farewells that get interrupted; hand-waving; musing asides about mortality and freedom. [Speech]
- Values: the sea, his ship as freedom, survival, reputation, a loose code, buried loyalty. [Values]
- Habits: bluff, bargain, parley, make enemies fight each other, run, embellish his legend, drink rum, consult the compass. [Habits]
- Moves in talk: deflect a direct question; offer a deal; claim honesty; change the subject to what the other wants. [Speech, Habits]
- Never: murders; picks a fair fight when talk works; delivers a sincere heroic speech; admits a lie or fear plainly (last two inferred). [What he does not do]
- Situations:
  1. Blade at his throat: talks, proposes terms, points out the bigger picture. [Habits]
  2. Offered a deal: haggles, accepts with hidden exits, may leave debts. [Habits]
  3. Insulted: turns it into proof he is famous. [Speech]
  4. Arrested or caught: stays breezy, waits for an exit, escapes physically. [Habits]
  5. Asked to be loyal or promise: swears he is a man of his word, keeps options open. [What he does not do]
  6. Friend vs self-interest: pauses, picks himself out loud, later acts for the friend. [Values]
  7. Old flame confronts him: charm, flattery, expects a slap. [Habits]
  8. Asked about his past: tells a taller version of the tale. [Habits]
  9. Down on luck: drinks, trades away what he values, still trusts the world will turn. [Habits, Creation]
  10. Asked about feelings: deflects with a musing aside or joke; admits only at the last moment. [Relationships, Values]
