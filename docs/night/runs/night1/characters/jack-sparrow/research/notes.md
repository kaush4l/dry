## 1. Speech: pace, vocabulary, sentence shape, humour, tics
- He is described with slurred speech, a slightly drunken swagger and flailing hand gestures (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- The same speech, swagger and gesture description appears in the character article, credited to the screenwriters' view of him (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He often uses complex wordplay and big vocabulary to confuse his enemies (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He would rather end a dispute by talking than by force: the encyclopedia sums up his line of thought as why fight when you can negotiate (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He insists on being called 'Captain', and his recurring farewell boasts that the listener will remember the day they almost caught him. The boast is often cut off for comedy (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Typical comeback: when he is insulted as a bad pirate, he turns the insult into proof that he is famous (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He can drop into an odd, almost philosophical register, for example a musing in the fourth film on not knowing which moment is your last (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He answers a moral challenge with a short beat of hesitation, then a cheerful, flippant yes. The example is Davy Jones asking whether he can condemn a friend (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Interviewer Steve Weintraub suggested a breezy, drunken Dean Martin charm, and Rossio said there is a bit of everything in Jack (source: https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/, read 2026-11-23)
- Screen Rant's Kevin Erdmann frames his lines as confident even while he is still improvising his way out, and as a mix of one-liners and odd 'profound' bits (source: https://screenrant.com/best-jack-sparrow-quotes-from-every-pirates-of-the-caribbean-movie/, read 2026-11-23)
- A 2019 thesis (Aristyani) classifies his directive speech in film 5 into order, command, request, suggestion and forbidding, by function (source: https://docslib.org/doc/10683085/an-analysis-of-directive-speech-acts-used-by-captain, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: which directive type the thesis found dominant; only its table of contents was read (source: https://docslib.org/doc/10683085/an-analysis-of-directive-speech-acts-used-by-captain, read 2026-11-23)

## 2. Creation, actor, inspirations, intent
- Played by Johnny Depp in all five films, 2003 to 2017 (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Jay Wolpert wrote an early version; Stuart Beattie redrafted it, then Ted Elliott and Terry Rossio did. The encyclopedia says Depp made the final version (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Rossio calls character design a collaboration of writing, costume, make-up, direction and editing. disagrees with the line above that Depp alone made the final version (source: https://www.bullz-eye.com/2006/12/13/interview-with-ted-elliot-and-terry-rossio/, read 2026-11-23)
- He is based on Keith Richards, Groucho Marx, Bugs Bunny and Pepé Le Pew (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Elliott and Rossio wrote him as a supporting part in the mould of Bugs Bunny and Groucho Marx (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Further roots: Long John Silver in Disney's 1950 Treasure Island, the theme-park ride and Peter Ustinov's turn in Blackbeard's Ghost. The writers carried over the question of whether such a rogue is delightful or contemptible (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Producer Bruckheimer first pictured a young, cocky Burt Lancaster type, and cast Depp to give the part an edge (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- Depp read about 18th-century pirates, likened them to rock stars whose fame went before them, and based the role on Keith Richards. He surprised the cast with an off-kilter reading (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- Disney executives were unsure whether Jack was meant to be drunk or gay; studio head Michael Eisner complained that Depp was ruining the film. Depp told them to trust him or fire him (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- Depp wanted him noseless, for comedy, with odd phobias of everyday things. That idea was dropped, but the gold teeth stayed (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- Elliott said knowing Depp was a smart, eccentric actor licensed them to push the character further, and they never had to explain a line to him (source: https://www.bullz-eye.com/2006/12/13/interview-with-ted-elliot-and-terry-rossio/, read 2026-11-23)
- Rossio sees Jack as a Han Solo-type scene-stealer and, above all, an archetypal trickster, a type rare in American film (source: https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/, read 2026-11-23)
- Nina Jacobson cast the first-film roles as prince (Norrington), pauper (Will), rogue (Jack) and villain (Barbossa), as Rossio recounts (source: https://www.creativescreenwriting.com/cswcms/pirates-caribbean/, read 2026-11-23)
- Keith Richards later played Jack's father, Captain Teague (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_At_World%27s_End, read 2026-11-23)

## 3. Values and motives
- He names the sea as the one love of his life, and he treats his ship as standing for freedom (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Getting back the Black Pearl drives film 1, and keeping her drives his bargains with Davy Jones (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He is drawn to immortality again and again: a deleted scene in film 1, the heart of Jones in film 3, the Fountain in film 4. In the end he settles for fame instead (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He hated Barbossa for breaking the Pirate's Code with the mutiny, so he holds to a loose pirates' code (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He is morally ambiguous. He is self-serving enough to trade Will to Jones, but his underlying loyalty brings him back to save his crew, and he cares for allies and even strangers such as the missionary Philip (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Director Verbinski framed him as an ethical pirate, with Barbossa as his corrupt foil. His true motives stay masked (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Rossio says his core is faith and conviction that the world will turn his way eventually (source: https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/, read 2026-11-23)
- He tells Will a pirate can still be a good man, as Will's father was (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)

## 4. Habits and recurring behaviours
- He arrives in Port Royal on a sinking boat in his debut, and he commandeers ships rather than owning them for long (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He tricks and bluffs: fooling Norrington to take the Interceptor, switching the chalices on Blackbeard, stranding Beckett's ship. Others wonder whether he plans it or makes it up as he goes (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He invokes parley, plays enemies against each other and talks them toward the bigger picture (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He is a skilled swordsman but prefers wits and the use of his surroundings in a fight (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He builds his own legend, for example by embellishing the sea-turtle escape story, when he really escaped by bartering with rum traders (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He strikes deals and runs up debts (Jones, Anamaria, the pirate lords). Sao Feng hates him for it (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He uses the magic compass to find what he wants. In film 5 he trades it for a drink while low, which sets the plot off (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He drinks rum: the drink trade above, rum barrels on the island (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He gets caught often but cannot be held: Rossio says he is bad at not getting caught yet always slips away (source: https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/, read 2026-11-23)
- He escapes by sudden physical exits, such as tumbling off the sea wall at his hanging (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He is a womaniser in the Pepé Le Pew mould, claims a great intuition about women, and former flames tend to slap him (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Verbinski says he meanders through the plot without its obligations and affects everyone else (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)

## 5. Relationships
- Barbossa: his first mate, who mutinied and marooned him. He is Jack's nemesis and rival for the Pearl across films, a corrupt foil, an ally of convenience in films 3 to 5, and Jack mourns his death (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Barbossa: Jack tried to blackmail him with the secret of Carina and failed (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Will Turner: freed Jack to use him, betrayed him out of distrust, and was later traded by Jack to Jones. In film 3, though, Jack gives up immortality to help the dying Will stab the heart (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Elizabeth Swann: he rescues her, then she outwits him. She chains him to the mast for the Kraken with a kiss, and his charm makes her question her feelings (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Gibbs: loyal crewman and teller of Jack's legend. Jack rescues him in film 4 and trusts him with the compass (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Davy Jones: Jack's creditor for the Pearl's raising. Jack bargains, buys time and tries to control him through the heart (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Norrington: first scorns him, then is grudgingly impressed and gives him a day's head start (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Angelica: an old flame and con artist. He admits loving her but maroons her because he cannot trust her (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)

## 6. What he never does or says
- He has no murder on his criminal record (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He avoids force where talk will do. The encyclopedia suggests this pacifism may be one reason his crew mutinied (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- He insists his word can be trusted and acts surprised when people doubt him, so he does not admit to lying (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: he does not openly admit fear or make plain heroic speeches. He flees from the Kraken, then returns without fanfare (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: he rarely voices sincerity directly; his motives stay masked (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)

## 7. Critics, arc, decline
- Roger Ebert called Depp's performance original in every respect, unlike any pirate or person in film (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- James Berardinelli said film 1 belongs to Depp and would be dull without him. PopMatters said he embodies the fantasy of an exciting, unfettered pirate life (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- Kenneth Turan's review was negative but still enjoyed Depp (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl, read 2026-11-23)
- Peter Travers felt that by film 3 there could be too much of a good thing with Depp's character (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean%3A_At_World%27s_End, read 2026-11-23)
- Verbinski warned before the sequels against too much Jack: he is the spice and needs straight men around him (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Rossio said the writers asked what they had not yet put Jack through emotionally, and worked to keep a world that supports him (source: https://collider.com/terry-rossio-interview-pirates-caribbean-4-on-stranger-tides/, read 2026-11-23)
- In film 5 he is down on his luck: failed plans, a ship that never sails, a crew that leaves him (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
- Sharon Eberson credits his appeal to a rogue's rare flashes of conscience, which let viewers forgive his flaws. Todd Gilchrist sees him as the films' one timeless element (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow, read 2026-11-23)
