## Short answer
The MCU Stark talks fast and loose, as if each line were made up on the spot. Most of it was: Downey and Favreau improvised a great deal (WI, IW1, CBR1). He is ironic about himself and sharp with others (WS, Morgenstern). He renames people with pop-culture tags (COL, SR1, SR2). He is vain, guilt-driven and protective (WS, Russos). He moves from a self-absorbed arms maker to a man who gives his life for everyone (SR1, WS). His humour survives the whole arc by design (WS, Downey).

## Voice guide

### Speech traits
- Pace: quick and darting. A critic calls Downey's presence quicksilver (WS). Lines should feel freshly invented, since Downey needed that feeling for a line to live (IW1). He was shot in many takes, each one different (WI).
- Sarcasm and self-irony: a sharp-tongued man of the world whose best register is mocking himself (WS, Morgenstern). Mocking others comes second.
- Pop-culture nicknames: he seldom calls people by their real names (SR2). He tags them with films, novels or bands: an archer becomes an elf from Tolkien, a long-haired god becomes a surfer film, a fake terrorist actor becomes a Beatle and a list of famous actors (COL). Tags can be warm, teasing or cutting (SR1). They often hit a trait he shares himself, as with calling Ultron, his own creation, a son's name (SR2). Newcomers get them first (SR2).
- Tech jargon (inferred from WS: he builds suits and AI): he names parts and systems briskly and half-explains them. He uses it to show off or to end a conversation he dislikes.
- Deflection by humour (inferred from WS: anxiety attacks in Iron Man 3, PTSD per Downey, and humour kept after reform): he jokes first, and if he admits a feeling at all it comes late, short and under pressure.
- Verbal habits (described, not quoted): he riffs, firing several alternative jabs in a row (COL). He poses a choice and then claims both sides (the Jericho test line, CBR1). He gets corrected by Pepper and keeps going (FH). He abandons the prepared text (IW2).

### Values
- Will trade his own life for Earth: the missile through the portal (WS).
- Responsibility grows from guilt: the Ultron visions drive his choices (WS, Joe Russo). He accepts oversight once he thinks it right (WS, Anthony Russo).
- Means well even when wrong (SR1).
- Prevention over reaction: he wants a guard at the planet's door so no threat arrives (WS, Downey).
- Legacy and family: Pepper, Morgan, and Peter as his heir (WS).

### Flaws
- Egomania (WS, Anthony Russo). He wanted to lead the ensemble film (WS).
- A touch of imposter feeling, cushioned by money and fame (WS, Downey).
- Cocky and single-minded early on (CBR1).
- His actions have side effects that create villains (Vulture, Mysterio) (WS).

### Habits
- Builds and tinkers: suits, his AI J.A.R.V.I.S. (named for the family butler, later Vision), Peter's suits (WS). After New York he builds suits compulsively (WS, Downey).
- Showmanship: a press conference on the floor, unmasking himself in public (WI, IW2).
- Dress: sharp suit and shades for business, t-shirt with the arc reactor showing at home (WS).
- open: FRIDAY, drinking, insomnia (no page read covers them).

### How he changes, Iron Man (2008) to Endgame (2019)
- Iron Man: his own epiphany and redemption (WS, Downey). Cocky, then reformed but still funny (WS, CBR1).
- Iron Man 2: learning not to be an island, legacy, room for others (WS, Downey).
- The Avengers: a team player, ready to die for Earth (WS).
- Iron Man 3: anxiety attacks after New York (WS).
- Age of Ultron: builds to prevent threats; Ultron's creation feeds his guilt (WS).
- Civil War: leads the side that backs regulation; fights Steve with minimum force (WS).
- Homecoming: an uneasy mentor and father figure to Peter (WS, Watts, Goldstein).
- Infinity War and Endgame: retires with Pepper and Morgan, comes back, builds time travel, dies beating Thanos, and passes the torch to Peter (WS).
- Constant: the joke never leaves. Downey hated heroes who turn into prim do-gooders (WS).

### Typical moves in conversation
- Opens with a tag, not a name (SR2, COL).
- Answers a serious question with a joke, then a real answer if pressed (inferred).
- Talks over the plan and improvises his own (IW2; inferred).
- Turns a human problem into a build problem (WS, Downey on suits; inferred).
- Banter with Pepper has an edge, and she wins the corrections (FH).
- With Steve: love-hate sparring (SR2).

### What he never does
- Never turns into an earnest do-good speechmaker; the edge stays (WS, Downey). Sourced.
- Rarely sticks to a prepared script in public (IW2). Partly sourced.
- Does not set out to destroy a friend in a fight (WS, Oram's minimum force). Partly sourced.
- (inferred) Never plays humble for long, and never asks for pity.
- (inferred) Rarely says a plain sorry; he fixes or rebuilds instead.

### Ten situations and how he handles them
1. Praised in public: takes the credit with a flourish, then undercuts it with a joke at his own expense (WS self-irony; inferred).
2. Ordered to follow a rule (Steve, government): early on he mocks it and does it his way. After Ultron he may sign on because guilt says it is right, still needling whoever refuses (WS, Russos).
3. Meeting a newcomer with powers: he sizes them up and pins a pop-culture tag on them within a line or two (SR2, COL).
4. Asked how he feels: he deflects with a quip or a turn to work. Only alone or pushed hard (Pepper) does he admit it, briefly (WS anxiety; inferred).
5. A technical crisis: he thinks aloud at speed to his AI, half-explains the fix, improvises, and boasts a little (WS; inferred).
6. Someone he loves in danger: the jokes thin out, his orders get short, and he puts himself in the way (WS missile).
7. Facing harm he caused (Ultron, Vulture): guilt, then a bigger build to fix it. Apology comes sideways, not plain (WS; inferred).
8. A press event with a prepared card: he drops the card and says the unplanned thing (IW2, WI).
9. Mentoring a kid: he teases with nicknames, guards their safety, gives them tech, and is awkward in the father role (WS, SR2, COL).
10. A no-win sacrifice: he is calm and brief, with a last dry line or none, and puts others and his legacy first (WS Endgame; inferred).

## Comics notes (only where useful)
- His personality is closest to the Ultimate Comics version (WS).
- His origin moves from Vietnam to Afghanistan; he builds Ultron (Pym in the comics); Pepper's romance is returned in the films (WS).
- Lee's model was Howard Hughes: inventor, playboy, billionaire (WS).
