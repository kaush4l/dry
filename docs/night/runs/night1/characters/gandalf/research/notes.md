# Notes: how Gandalf speaks, counsels and behaves

All pages were read on 2026-11-23. Wikipedia (429) and every web search (DuckDuckGo 403 or no results) failed, so the sources are Tolkien Gateway, the Encyclopedia of Arda (glyphweb) and one Gateway page quoting McKellen. ## 1. How his speech is described (register, vocabulary, sentence shape, humour, temper, habits)
- The book's own description, as summarised by the Gateway, is that he is quick to anger and just as quick to laugh (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- The Gateway says he was alternately affectionate and brusque, and often surprised people with bluntness when time was short (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He regularly scolded foolish behaviour, but rewarded generously those who acted with good intent (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- A lament for him in the book, quoted on the Gateway, sets a voice like a trumpet and a lordly wisdom beside a tired old pilgrim in a battered hat: the high and the homely side by side (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- When angered or roused for battle he seemed to grow taller and take on a terrifying look (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Both encyclopedias say his eyes, under heavy brows, could suddenly blaze like fire, and observers sensed a veiled power in them (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- The Encyclopedia of Arda gives the same picture of his eyes and of more power held in reserve than he ever showed (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)
- McKellen, quoted on the Gateway, said Tolkien's own recorded readings encouraged him: they were theatrical, full of rhythm, humour and characterisation. He also said Gandalf is like Tolkien (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Ian_McKellen, read 2026-11-23)
- Saruman's great power was a voice of quiet, measured, reasonable-sounding persuasion. This is the opposite pole to Gandalf, whose sway comes from counsel rather than spell-like speech (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/s/saruman.html, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: the Gateway text suggests his speech mixes the grand register (proclamation at Aragorn's crowning, in high ceremonial phrasing about the Valar) with plain, curt, practical talk on the road. No page analyses his diction directly (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)

## 2. His nature according to Tolkien (Maia, Olórin, mission, limits, inspirations)
- He was a Maia, the wisest of them, made before the world. In Valinor he was called Olórin and was a counsellor of Irmo in Lórien (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He often went to Nienna, and pity and patience were what her teaching gave him (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- The Gateway lists him as serving Manwë, Varda, Irmo and Nienna; this differs from (does not flatly contradict) the Arda line below (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- The Encyclopedia of Arda says he first belonged among the Maiar of Manwë and Varda; this disagrees with the Gateway's wider list (Irmo, Nienna) (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)
- He was one of five Istari the Valar sent about a thousand years into the Third Age, to help the free peoples resist Sauron (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/w/wizards.html, read 2026-11-23)
- Istar is a Quenya word for 'wise one', or 'those who know', per Letter 156 (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Istari, read 2026-11-23)
- In Letter 181 Tolkien says the Valar sent the wizards to help those who stood against Sauron (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_181, read 2026-11-23)
- In Letter 156 Tolkien insists Gandalf truly died and was changed, since treating death as making no difference would be the real cheat (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_156, read 2026-11-23)
- Tolkien admits the manner of Gandalf's return is a narrative defect forced by technique (Letter 156) (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_156, read 2026-11-23)
- In Letter 155 Tolkien says the supremely bad motive is the domination of other free wills. Gandalf and the Elves use real magic sparingly and only for specific good ends, such as lighting fire in wet wood (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_155, read 2026-11-23)
- Círdan gave him Narya, the Ring of Fire, at the Havens. Círdan said it would support him in the weariness he had taken on and help kindle hearts (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Narya, read 2026-11-23)
- Arda confirms that Círdan gave him Narya to help him contest Sauron's will (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)
- Tolkien called him an 'Odinic wanderer', and a postcard of a painting, Der Berggeist, is cited as a visual source (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Unlike Saruman and Radagast he never settled anywhere, and wandered for some fifteen centuries (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)
- His Elvish name Mithrandir means 'Grey Wanderer' (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/w/wizards.html, read 2026-11-23)
- He has many names, including Stormcrow, Láthspell, Grey Pilgrim, Grey Fool and Incánus. Several of these are insults that show how bringers of bad news are received (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/m/mithrandir.html, read 2026-11-23)

## 3. Grey versus White (books and films)
- As the Grey he ranked second to Saruman. After Moria he came back clad in white, now leading the order (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Sent back by Eru as the only Istar true to his errand, he was allowed to show more of his inner power. He seldom used it, since his mission was still to support others, but when roused few servants of Sauron could stand him (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Arda agrees that after his return as the White he had far more power than he ever displayed (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)
- Letter 156: the return was a real change, not a mere revival (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_156, read 2026-11-23)
- In the films, the Gateway notes that the White in The Two Towers is played as more vigorous and commanding, and that in The Return of the King he leads troops in battle (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)

## 4. How he counsels: hope, pity, mercy, free will
- When Frodo said Gollum deserved death, Gandalf answered that Gollum might yet have a part to play, and that dealing out life and death was not a choice Frodo should make (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Tolkien (Letter 181) says Frodo's earlier mercy to Gollum is what in the end destroyed the Ring, which Frodo could not do alone (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_181, read 2026-11-23)
- Tolkien (Letter 246) adds that the failure was redeemed by that mercy and by grace. He says we may hold ourselves to high ideals but should soften how we judge other people with mercy (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_246, read 2026-11-23)
- He suggested that Frodo leave the Shire, and let Frodo set the date and the cover story. He advises; the other person decides (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- His chief way of working against Sauron was encouraging others and giving wisdom at key moments, not acting by force himself (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- In besieged Minas Tirith he urged the men to hope, and his presence alone lifted the fear of the Ringwraiths (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- At the Black Gate he advised the lords to make a near-hopeless march, drawing Sauron's eye from Frodo: he would gamble everything on the small hidden chance (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- When Barliman confessed that the hobbits had left with a suspicious Ranger, Gandalf saw it as a hope beyond what he had expected and congratulated the puzzled innkeeper (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He had great pity and patience, even toward the servants of his enemies (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)

## 5. Habits and signature traits
- Hobbits taught him to smoke pipe-weed, and he was known for puffing smoke-rings. Saruman mocked the habit, then secretly took it up (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Pipe-weed, read 2026-11-23)
- Of all the wizards, he was the one most drawn to hobbits, and he went to the Shire for rest between errands. The Gateway guesses he was amused by them, or liked that they were untouched by great evil (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He used fire as a weapon in small, inventive ways, for example turning pine cones into flaming missiles against wolves (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- His mark is the rune G, which he scratched on stones at Weathertop for his friends to find (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He wrote Frodo a letter with a riddle, so that Frodo could tell the true Strider (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He tamed Shadowfax by following him for two days across the fields. The horse carried him through the war and went with him to the Havens (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Shadowfax, read 2026-11-23)
- Arda describes his dress: a long grey cloak and a tall pointed blue hat with a wide brim (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)

## 6. How he treats key people
- Frodo: he told him plainly that the Ring was the One, guided him toward Rivendell and would not let him judge Gollum (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Pippin: he took Pippin to Minas Tirith as an ironic 'reward' for gazing into the palantír. He scolds the fool but keeps him close (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Aragorn: he trusted him with the hobbits and was glad when they met. At the coronation he set the crown on Aragorn's head at Aragorn's request and blessed the new age (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Saruman: Gandalf rejected with horror Saruman's offer to take the Ring and seize power together (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Saruman later refused Gandalf's offer of forgiveness at Orthanc. Gandalf then shattered his staff and expelled him from the order, and later offered help again on the road (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Saruman, read 2026-11-23)
- Gandalf described Saruman fairly even before the betrayal: great in knowledge, but with his pride grown alongside it and resentful of meddling (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Saruman, read 2026-11-23)
- Saruman envied Gandalf (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Saruman, read 2026-11-23)
- Denethor seemed more wizard-like in look than Gandalf himself, and treated him with suspicion and disrespect. Gandalf still worked beside him (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Denethor_II, read 2026-11-23)
- When Denethor despaired, Gandalf took over the city's defence and ran to stop the pyre. He saved Faramir but could not save Denethor (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Théoden, weakened by Saruman's spells and Gríma, was roused by Gandalf. Gandalf drove off Wormtongue and urged the king to ride to war (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Théoden's decline is put down to Saruman's spells, perhaps also to age and poison (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden, read 2026-11-23)
- Gollum: Gandalf regretted neglecting the trail for years, questioned him for days, and still argued for pity (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- The hobbits at the end: he left them at the Shire's border to settle their own troubles, since such matters were no longer his to fix (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- OPEN: Bilbo is covered only as plot (the party, the Ring); no page read describes their friendship in tone.

## 7. What he never does and the temptations he refuses
- He refused Saruman's offer to share the Ring and power (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- Tolkien (Letter 246): had Gandalf taken the Ring and won, it would have been worse than Sauron winning. He would have grown self-righteous and run other people's lives for what he judged their good (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_246, read 2026-11-23)
- Tolkien (Letter 155): dominating other free wills is the worst motive, which is why Gandalf's magic is sparing and done for others (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_155, read 2026-11-23)
- He rarely unveiled his full power even as the White, because his task was to support and succour, not to rule (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He did not chase the Witch-king for glory but turned at once to save Faramir (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, read 2026-11-23)
- He never settled or took a domain of his own (source: https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html, read 2026-11-23)

## 8. McKellen on playing him
- McKellen, on the Gateway page, links his Gandalf to Tolkien's own reading style (rhythm, humour, theatricality) and calls Gandalf like Tolkien (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Ian_McKellen, read 2026-11-23)
- He describes Gandalf and Magneto as two iconic men of power played back to back (source: https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Ian_McKellen, read 2026-11-23)
