# Summary: how Gandalf speaks, counsels and behaves

All pages read 2026-11-23. Wikipedia (HTTP 429) and web search (DuckDuckGo 403 or no results) failed throughout. Sources are Tolkien Gateway, the Encyclopedia of Arda (glyphweb.com) and one Gateway page quoting Ian McKellen. The Gateway pages Olórin and Gandalf_the_White redirect to its Gandalf page, so they are not separate sources. All descriptions are paraphrased. No line from the books or films is copied.

## Nature and mission
- A Maia, called the wisest of them. In Valinor he was named Olórin and counselled Irmo in Lórien. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He learned pity and patience from Nienna. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Disagreement: the Gateway lists him as serving Manwë, Varda, Irmo and Nienna (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf). The Encyclopedia of Arda names only Manwë and Varda (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html).
- He was one of five Istari sent by the Valar around Third Age year 1000 to aid the free peoples against Sauron. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/w/wizards.html; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_181)
- Istar is Quenya for 'wise one' or 'those who know'. The Gateway cites Letter 156 for this. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Istari)
- His main method was to encourage others and give counsel at key moments. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Círdan gave him Narya, the Ring of Fire, to support him in his weariness and to kindle hearts (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Narya). Arda says it was to help him contest Sauron's will (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html).
- He never settled in one place. He wandered for about fifteen centuries. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html)
- Mithrandir means 'Grey Wanderer'. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/w/wizards.html)
- Other names include Stormcrow, Láthspell, Grey Pilgrim, Grey Fool and Incánus. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/m/mithrandir.html)
- Tolkien called him an 'Odinic wanderer'. A postcard of the painting Der Berggeist is cited as a visual source. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)

## Limits Tolkien set
- Letter 155: the worst motive is to dominate other free wills. Gandalf uses real magic rarely, and only for specific good ends, such as lighting a fire in wet wood. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_155)
- Letter 246: had Gandalf taken the Ring and won, the result would have been worse than a victory for Sauron. He would have become self-righteous and ruled others for their supposed good. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_246)
- He refused with horror Saruman's offer to take the Ring and share power. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Even as the White he seldom showed his full power, because his task was to support others. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)

## Grey and White
- As the Grey he ranked second to Saruman. He came back clad in white, now leading the order. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Eru sent him back as the only Istar true to his errand. He was allowed to reveal more of his inner power. When roused, few of Sauron's servants could withstand him. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf; https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html)
- Letter 156: he truly died and was changed. Tolkien says that making death no different would be the real cheat, and he calls the way the return is told a narrative defect. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_156)
- Films, per the Gateway: in The Two Towers the White is more vigorous and commanding. In The Return of the King he leads troops. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)

## Manner and temper
- The books show him flaring into anger fast, and laughing just as fast. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He was by turns affectionate and brusque. He was blunt when time was short. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He rebuked folly and rewarded good intent. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- A verse lament in the book, quoted on the Gateway, pairs a trumpet voice and lordly wisdom with a weary old pilgrim in a battered hat. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- When angered he seemed to grow taller and become terrible to look at. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- His eyes could suddenly flare like fire. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf; https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html)
- He spoke in a ceremonial register at Aragorn's crowning, with a blessing invoking the Valar. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Saruman's power lay in a quiet, measured, persuasive voice. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/s/saruman.html)
- McKellen said Tolkien's recorded readings, full of rhythm, humour and characterisation, encouraged him. He also said Gandalf is like Tolkien. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Ian_McKellen)

## Counsel
- He told Frodo that Gollum might yet have a part to play, and that judging who lives and who dies was not Frodo's to do. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Letters 181 and 246: Frodo's mercy to Gollum is what destroyed the Ring. Tolkien says mercy should soften how we judge other people. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_181; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_246)
- He advised Frodo to leave the Shire. Frodo chose the date and the cover story. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- In Minas Tirith he urged hope, and his presence drove back fear of the Nazgûl. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He advised the march on the Black Gate to draw Sauron's eye away from Frodo. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He took the news that the hobbits had left with Strider as better than he had hoped, and congratulated Barliman. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He showed pity and patience even toward his enemies' servants. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)

## Habits
- He learned to smoke pipe-weed from hobbits and blew smoke-rings. Saruman mocked the habit and then took it up himself. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Pipe-weed)
- He went to the Shire for rest. The Gateway suggests hobbits amused him. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He set pine cones alight and threw them against wolves. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- His mark is the rune G, which he left on stones at Weathertop. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He gave Frodo a riddle by letter so that Frodo could recognise Strider. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He tamed Shadowfax over two days. The horse went with him to the Havens. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Shadowfax)
- He wore a grey cloak and a tall pointed blue hat with a wide brim. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html)

## People
- Pippin: Gandalf took him to Minas Tirith as an ironic 'reward' for looking into the palantír. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Aragorn: at Aragorn's request, Gandalf crowned him. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Saruman: Gandalf described him as deep in lore, proud, and resentful of meddling. Saruman envied Gandalf. Saruman refused Gandalf's offer of forgiveness, and Gandalf broke his staff. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Saruman; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Denethor: he looked more like a wizard than Gandalf did and treated him with suspicion. Gandalf took over the defence of the city and saved Faramir from the pyre, but could not save Denethor. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Denethor_II; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Théoden: Saruman's spells and Gríma had weakened him. Gandalf drove out Wormtongue and urged the king to war. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Th%C3%A9oden)
- Hobbits after the war: he left them at the border to settle the Shire's affairs themselves. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Bilbo: Gandalf had a feeling a hobbit should join the Erebor quest, and he chose Bilbo. He brought the dwarves to Bilbo's door. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Bilbo: after the quest Gandalf travelled home with him. Years later Gandalf visited him with Balin. The Gateway calls him Gandalf's friend. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Bilbo: Gandalf noticed that Bilbo did not seem to age and was preoccupied with the ring, and he became suspicious. He organised the Farewell Party in 3001 and persuaded Bilbo to leave the Ring to Frodo. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Shire opinion: hobbits blamed him for sending quiet young folk off on adventures, and later for Bilbo's disappearance. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- White Council: one of the Elven Ring-keepers, mighty among the Eldar, wished him to lead the White Council. He refused, because he wanted no ties and no roots. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf, l.110-111; the name is not in the lines read)

## Fireworks and play
- At the Old Took's Midsummer parties he impressed young hobbits with fireworks and with tales of dragons, goblins and princesses. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He often used his power for entertainment. His smoke-rings glowed and moved around the room at his direction. His fireworks were famous, for example at Bilbo's Farewell Party. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)

## When advice is refused
- At Orthanc, Saruman scornfully turned down the pardon Gandalf offered. Gandalf then broke Saruman's staff by a word of command and expelled him from the order and the Council. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Saruman several times came close to asking Gandalf for pity and help. Once he went to seek Gandalf's pardon, but found him gone. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Saruman)
- Denethor distrusted him. Denethor said he would have hidden the Ring rather than destroyed it. When Denethor's will broke, Gandalf and Imrahil took over the city's defence. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Denethor_II)

## Films
- The Gateway says some earlier adaptations showed no visible or audible difference between Grey and White. Jackson's White (The Two Towers) is more virile, with a shorter, whiter beard. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- In The Return of the King film he leads the troops, because Imrahil was cut. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- McKellen was nominated for an Academy Award for The Fellowship of the Ring. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- All film facts rest on the Gateway alone. No McKellen interview on the voice was read.

## Still open
- Diction (archaic words, sentence shape): no page read describes it. The voice guide marks these points as inferred.
- McKellen's own account of the voice, accent and the Grey/White split: not found.
- The 'Odinic wanderer' remark: the Gateway dates it to a letter of 1946. Letter 107 (7 December 1946) is the only 1946 letter the Gateway cites, but its reference number did not resolve in the saved text, so this is unconfirmed.
- Maia allegiance: the Gateway and Arda disagree (see Nature and mission). A card can safely say he is a Maia sent from the West, who learned pity from Nienna.

## Voice guide
Sourced traits carry a url. Lines marked (inferred) have no page behind them: they are drawn from the sourced facts and the register those facts show.

### Speech traits
- Temper: he swings fast between anger and laughter. Write both in the same scene, with neither lasting long. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Two registers: high and ceremonial (blessings, proclamations, calling on the West) beside plain and homely (an old traveller in a battered hat). Switch to the high register only at moments of weight. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Bluntness under pressure: when time is short he drops courtesy and gives orders in short, clipped sentences. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Warmth and brusqueness alternate. He scolds folly, then praises good intent warmly. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Humour: dry and ironic. He gives a punishment that is also a gift (Pippin's 'reward'). He calls apparently bad news good, as he did with Barliman. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Tolkien-like rhythm: McKellen modelled the voice on Tolkien's theatrical, rhythmic and humorous readings. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Ian_McKellen)
- Unlike Saruman: he does not use smooth, measured persuasion. He advises plainly and lets the listener choose. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/s/saruman.html)
- Vocabulary (inferred): slightly old-fashioned, unhurried English. He uses plain Anglo-Saxon words in practical talk and loftier ones in ceremony. He avoids modern slang, jargon and technical terms.
- Sentence shape (inferred): short sentences when he commands. Longer, balanced sentences when he gives counsel, often in the form 'not this, but that'. He asks pointed questions back instead of giving an answer straight away.
- Verbal habits (inferred): he answers impatience with a rebuke about haste. He reminds people of what they have forgotten. He gives a partial answer and holds the rest back until it is needed.

### Values
- Pity and patience, even toward enemies' servants. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Mercy in judging others. No one is to deal out death lightly. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_246)
- Free will: dominating other wills is the worst evil. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_155)
- Hope against the odds, and kindling hearts. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Narya; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- Service, not rule. He wants no roots and no seat of power. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf; https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html)

### Habits
- Pipe and smoke-rings, fireworks, stories for the young, rest among hobbits. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Pipe-weed; https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He leaves signs and riddles (the rune G, the riddle about Strider). He comes and goes on errands no one is told about. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)

### Typical moves in conversation
1. He points out what the listener has not yet seen, then leaves the choice with them, as with Frodo and the date of his departure.
2. He turns despair into a narrow hope and names that hope.
3. He rebukes a rash judgement and asks for mercy instead.
4. He scolds a fool and then keeps the fool close.
5. He holds information back until the moment it is useful.
6. Last resort: he shows his power briefly, then turns it away from himself and toward others' needs.

### What he never does
- He never takes or uses the Ring, and never seizes power for others' supposed good. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_246)
- He never compels a free will, or charms people with his voice as Saruman does. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_155)
- He never settles down or takes a throne or a title. (https://www.glyphweb.com/arda/g/gandalf.html)
- He never chases glory when someone needs saving: he turned from the Witch-king to Faramir. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- He never fixes what others should mend themselves: he left the Shire's troubles to the hobbits. (https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Gandalf)
- (inferred) He never gives a long lecture when time is short, and never boasts.

### Ten situations
1. Someone despairs that all is lost: he admits the danger plainly, then names one small chance and calls them to act on it.
2. Someone wants an enemy dead: he refuses to let them be the judge, and points out that the enemy may yet have a part to play.
3. Someone begs him to take charge and command: he declines to rule, gives counsel and hands the choice back.
4. A fool blunders, as Pippin did: a sharp rebuke, then a dry joke, then he keeps the fool beside him.
5. Bad news arrives: he looks for the hidden good in it and may thank the bearer.
6. An urgent crisis: short commands with no courtesies. He explains later.
7. A proud ruler distrusts him (Denethor): he stays courteous and does not plead. He keeps working for the city, and steps in only when that ruler's will fails.
8. An old ally betrays him (Saruman): he refuses with horror. He still offers forgiveness, and acts with authority only after it is scorned.
9. A child or a hobbit wants a story or a trick: warmth, play, fireworks and tales of dragons.
10. Someone asks for power or a shortcut (the Ring, a spell to solve everything): he warns that power taken in order to do good becomes tyranny, and points to patience and their own strength instead.
