# Notes: Sherlock Holmes (Doyle canon), voice and method

All pages read 2026-11-23. Web search was mostly down (DuckDuckGo empty or 403); Gutenberg 503 and Britannica 403, so the canon is checked through two Wikisource chapters (A Study in Scarlet I.2, The Sign of the Four ch. 1) and the Arthur Conan Doyle Encyclopedia, a canon-only reference that cites story codes. No critic's description of his speech was found: that part rests on the primary chapters and quoted dialogue in Wikipedia.

## 1. Speech: pace, vocabulary, sentence shape, wit, verbal habits
- When asked how he knew something, he calls it simple (phrases such as simplicity itself) and then lays out the chain step by step, ending on the conclusion (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- His explanations name concrete physical evidence in exact detail (counting scratches on a shoe, saying where the firelight falls) before he states what follows from it (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- His humour is dry and mock-grand: he describes a careless servant girl in pompous, pseudo-scientific words, as if she were a specimen (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He tells of his own violence with relish and understatement: after a pub brawl he calls the fight delightful, sums it up in boxing terms and ends on a flat, comic line about the loser going home in a cart (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He sometimes calls a conclusion elementary and sometimes addresses Watson as my dear Watson, but the joined phrase Elementary, my dear Watson never occurs in the sixty stories (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- The joined catchphrase grew from William Gillette's 1899 play (in some versions) and was spread by the 1939-46 Rathbone films (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He separates his terms carefully in speech: he tells Watson what part of an inference is observation and what part is deduction (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- He uses the word data for evidence and complains when there is too little of it (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- When he thinks aloud his gaze drifts: Watson describes him staring at the ceiling with dreamy, dull eyes while he gives his reading of the watch (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- He calls his own learned writings trifling monographs, a modest word for exact and specialised work (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He admits to being unsociable and says he never mixed much with the men of his year at college (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: no critic or scholar describing his speech (pace, sentence length, register) was found; the traits above are drawn from his quoted lines only. OPEN.

## 2. His method as he explains it
- He reads people from dress and bearing: tattoos, ink stains, clay on boots, emotional state and health, wear on clothes and objects such as sticks, pipes and hats (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- In The Sign of the Four he reads a smear of reddish earth on Watson's instep, ties it to roadworks outside one post office, and gets to the telegram Watson sent by ruling out every other errand (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- Watson tests him with a watch that once belonged to Watson's brother; Holmes first protests that cleaning has removed most of the evidence, then reads the brother's life from scratches and pawnbroker marks (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- Watson at first takes the watch reading as a cruel trick, because the facts are painful family history; Holmes says he works from small facts on which large inferences depend (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: in the same scene Holmes apologises, saying he treated it as an abstract problem and forgot it was personal; the lines were not checked in the file. Check.
- His guiding rule, stated in The Sign of Four: once the impossible is eliminated, whatever remains must be the truth, however unlikely (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He warns that theorising before having data is a capital mistake, because one then bends facts to fit the theory (A Scandal in Bohemia) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- The stories call his method deduction, but it is mostly abduction (inferring the best explanation from details), with some true deduction as well (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He writes that a logician could infer an ocean or a great waterfall from one drop of water (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Mind-as-attic: he holds that a fool fills his brain with lumber of every kind, that the brain's room is limited, and that useless facts push out useful ones, so he chooses what he learns (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- He tells Watson he is a consulting detective: police and private agents bring him their tangles and he puts them right, helped by his knowledge of the history of crime and a family likeness among misdeeds (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- He relies on hard evidence as much as on reasoning: footprints, hoof and tyre marks, tobacco ash, handwriting, typewriting, gunpowder residue, magnifying glass at the scene, microscope and chemistry at home (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He uses applied psychology: he fakes a fire so Irene Adler shows where the photograph is hidden, and gets a salesman to talk by offering a bet, since a sporting man will tell more to win a wager than for money (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Maria Konnikova argues he gives his whole attention to a single task and seldom splits it, a habit she compares to mindfulness (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Doyle makes him fallible; The Yellow Face turns on his being wrong (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He calls Dupin's mind-reading trick showy and superficial, yet later performs the same trick on Watson (The Cardboard Box, The Dancing Men) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)

## 3. What he never does; his values
- He will not theorise ahead of the facts (see the capital-mistake rule) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He will not learn what does not serve his work; he tells Watson he will forget that the earth goes round the sun now that he knows it, because it is of no use to him (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- He does not chase public credit: he regularly lets Lestrade and other police take credit for his solutions (Empty House, Norwood Builder) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_Lestrade, read 2026-11-23)
- He refused a royal reward in A Scandal in Bohemia and asked only for Irene Adler's photograph (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Irene_Adler, read 2026-11-23)
- Watson reports he has an aversion to women and distrusts them, yet treats them with marked gentleness and courtesy, which is why Mrs Hudson is fond of him (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He goes without food during intense work, holding that starving sharpens the faculties (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- His mind rebels against stagnation; without a problem to work on he turns to cocaine, which Watson objects to (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: justice over law (letting some culprits go) is widely said of him, but no page read here states it. OPEN.

## 4. Habits and tastes
- Watson's list from A Study in Scarlet: knowledge of literature, philosophy and astronomy nil; politics feeble; botany patchy but strong on poisons; geology practical (tells soils apart by the splashes); chemistry profound; anatomy accurate but unsystematic; sensational crime literature immense; plays the violin well; good practical grasp of British law (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- Watson is astonished that Holmes knows nothing of Carlyle and does not know the earth goes round the sun (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- Later he describes himself as reading everything and remembering it unusually well (Lion's Mane); Doyle said he started him as a mere reasoning device and humanised him over the series (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He plays the violin remarkably but eccentrically, and relaxes with music, playing or at concerts (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- He is a cryptanalyst, author of a short monograph on 160 ciphers (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He is untidy with papers and lets documents pile up, but is clean about his person, cat-like in Watson's words, with a quiet primness of dress (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- His rooms are untidy beyond any standard of the time (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He lives at 221B Baker Street; the rooms were two bedrooms and one large, bright sitting room with two broad windows, shared with Watson to split the rent (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet/Part_1/Chapter_2, read 2026-11-23)
- Looks: six feet or more, so lean he seems taller; sharp, piercing grey eyes that turn far-away and inward when he works hardest (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He is a master of disguise, so good that Watson fails to recognise him, and he fakes illness or injury to trap the guilty (Dying Detective, Scandal) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He uses agents: the gossip Langdale Pike, the underworld contact Shinwell Johnson, and the street boys he calls the Baker Street Irregulars (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He is physically strong and a good boxer, fencer and singlestick player; he carries a cane or riding crop, and laughingly straightens a poker a bully has bent (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He was awarded the Legion of Honour for an arrest in Paris (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)

## 5. The people around him
- Watson: an army doctor wounded by a Jezail bullet in Afghanistan, middle-sized and sturdy; he married Mary Morstan, a client from The Sign of Four (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Dr._Watson, read 2026-11-23)
- Watson is the companion who is less clever than Holmes and has solutions explained to him; Doyle made this a fixed convention of the genre (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Holmes performs deductions on Watson and teases him with them, and Watson sometimes takes offence, as with the watch (source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sign_of_the_Four/Chapter_1, read 2026-11-23)
- Mycroft: seven years older, stout, with an even greater gift for observation and deduction but no ambition or energy; he walks between Pall Mall, Whitehall and the Diogenes Club and nowhere else (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Mycroft_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Lestrade: a determined but conventional Scotland Yard man who consults Holmes often; Holmes rates him and Gregson as quick and energetic but shockingly conventional, the best of a bad lot (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_Lestrade, read 2026-11-23)
- Lestrade at first doubts Holmes's methods and calls him too cocksure (Norwood Builder) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_Lestrade, read 2026-11-23)
- Irene Adler appears in one story only, yet she is one of the few people, and the only woman, to beat Holmes in a contest of wits; afterwards he refers to her only by an honourable title (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- He keeps the sovereign she gave him on his watch-chain (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Irene_Adler, read 2026-11-23)
- Moriarty: a tall, thin, pale mathematician (a famed treatise on the binomial theorem, The Dynamics of an Asteroid) turned criminal; Holmes treats him as an intellectual equal (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Professor_Moriarty, read 2026-11-23)
- Holmes says he threw Moriarty into the Reichenbach Falls using baritsu, a Japanese wrestling art (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- Clients: his clients range from modest private people to royalty (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_%281984_TV_series%29, read 2026-11-23)
- unconfirmed: his manner with clients scene by scene (greeting, reading them, sharp questions) was not found in a page read here. OPEN.

## 6. How Doyle built him
- Joseph Bell, an Edinburgh surgeon, taught students to look closely, reason sharply and then check against evidence, a practice he named The Method; he read strangers' jobs and recent movements in lectures, for example a woman's visit to the Botanic Garden from red clay on her shoes (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bell, read 2026-11-23)
- Doyle met Bell in 1877 and clerked for his outpatients at Edinburgh's Royal Infirmary (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bell, read 2026-11-23)
- Poe's Dupin used ratiocination mixed with imagination, living with a narrator friend; Watson compares Holmes to Dupin and Holmes is dismissive (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Auguste_Dupin, read 2026-11-23)
- Gaboriau's Lecoq, a methodical police detective, influenced Holmes; Holmes calls him a miserable bungler in A Study in Scarlet (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsieur_Lecoq, read 2026-11-23)
- Doyle first named the pair Sherrinford Holmes and Ormond Sacker (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)

## 7. Adaptations against the canon
- The Granada series with Jeremy Brett (1984-94) adapted 43 of the 60 stories in 41 episodes, kept the Victorian setting, and Sherlockians widely call it the most faithful (source: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/index.php/Jeremy_Brett, read 2026-11-23)
- The same series figures: 36 hour-long episodes and five feature-length specials (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_%281984_TV_series%29, read 2026-11-23)
- Basil Rathbone's 14 films (1939-46) began in the Victorian era at Fox, then moved to Universal's World War II present with Holmes fighting Nazis (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_%281939_film_series%29, read 2026-11-23)
- The Rathbone films spread the Elementary, my dear Watson phrase that the canon never uses (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes, read 2026-11-23)
- The BBC Sherlock (Cumberbatch, Moffat and Gatiss, 2010-17) is set in the present day (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_%28TV_series%29, read 2026-11-23)
